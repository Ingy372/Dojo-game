## 1. OV-C2 Blessing in runs: pass with 2 changes

There are no hours, no countdown, and nothing is lost by missing class. This matches the no-pressure rule. The count goes down only by playing, never by waiting, so it isn't a clock.

| Line | Verdict |
|---|---|
| "Dojo Blessing!" | Pass |
| "Dojo Blessing for your next runs!" | **Change** to "Dojo Blessing for your next five runs!" (7 words). A 6-year-old needs the number. "Next runs" is vague. |
| "Blessing on for {n} more runs." / "…one more run." | Pass |
| "Class time helps your next five runs!" | **Change in logic, not words:** don't show this line when the Blessing is already at 10. A class adds nothing then, so show the full line instead. Otherwise the game promises something it doesn't give. |
| "Blessing ready for ten Tower runs." | Pass |

Also: when the Blessing runs out, show nothing. No "Blessing ended" line, so there's no loss message.

## 2. OV-C2 belt entry: pass with 2 changes

- The parent note and the fix path are plain and calm. Pass.
- **Change:** on the confirm screen, use "Keep {belt}" instead of "Not yet". "Not yet" suggests the fix should happen later. It's the house hopeful phrase, but this is a yes/no choice.
- **Gap:** there's no line for the child when a fix moves the belt down. Suggestion: "Your belt was updated." (4 words), with no blame, and everything found and earned stays. The Content Designer can word it. A child should never read a fix as "you lost a belt."
- On the PIN question (DECIDE in OV-C2): many 8–9-year-olds can solve a sum question, so a PIN is the stronger gate. That is the Manager's or Jay's call. Learning-wise, either works for now.

## 3. Auto-equip "new move" line (OV-B1): proposed words

This shows inside the sign-off celebration that already exists. It isn't a new screen, so it doesn't use up the one-new-thing-per-session budget.

| When | Line | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Free slot | "Sensei added {move} to your moves!" | 6 |
| Slots full, oldest swapped out | "{move} is in. {old} is resting." | 6 |
| Picking is off (player chooses) | "{move} is ready. Add it anytime!" | 6 |

Use "resting", never "removed" or "lost". The old move is still owned and one tap away. Avoid the word "loadout" in kid lines.

Naming note for the Programmer: review 6.3 says "unless the player has turned picking on", but the switch is Sensei's Pick, where *on* means auto. Use one meaning everywhere: Sensei's Pick on = the game picks.

## 4. Sensei's Lesson for Sensei's Pick: proposed

The lesson fires the first time a sign-off auto-equips a move. It follows the usual pattern: one screen, then a try.
- Lesson: "Sensei picks your newest moves for you."
- Try it now: "Try your new move on this one."
- In class too: "You earned this move in class."
- After the try: "Nice! That's your new move."
- Retry, unchanged: "Nice work. Let's keep practicing."

On Guided (ages 6–9) the lesson never mentions the switch. The "pick for yourself" switch gets its own later lesson at Green or when the loadout opens (review 6.4). That keeps choices away from the youngest players without hiding them for good.
