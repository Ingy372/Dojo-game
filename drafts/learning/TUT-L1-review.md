## Short answers

1. **One skill per beat?** Yes. It is the right rule for 6-9s. Learner-paced, small segments help beginners learn (segmenting meta-analysis: Rey et al. 2019, *Educ Psychol Rev* 31:389-419). **MODERATE**: those studies are mostly lessons on screens with older students, not games, but the direction is consistent. The plan already pairs each skill with a "Got it" pause that waits for the kid, which is the learner-paced part.
2. **Is 6-8 minutes okay in one sitting?** Yes, keep it in one sitting. It is a run's length (runs are 5-7 min, Jay's choice), and every beat is play, not reading. The 5 rooms are resume points, so a kid who stops loses little. Only use the fallback (end after beat 6) if TUT-Q1 shows Guided kids over about 10 minutes (the plan's +30%). Evidence on "how long can a 6-year-old play a tutorial" is **WEAK**: I know of no good study, so TUT-Q1's timing is the real test.
3. **Are the stones scary?** Not if they look and sound like a game, not a cave-in. The TUT-C1 lines are gentle. See beat 2 for the 5 safety rules. A short stumble with stars and no damage is gentle and fine.

## The one real catch: the red circle means two things

Beat 2 teaches "red circle = step out". Beat 7 teaches "red circle = tap COUNTER". Both are true in the game (stepping out of the Brute's circle also avoids the hit), but a 6-year-old who just learned "get out" may run from the Brute and never try Counter. The guided Counter holds the game still, so they won't be stuck, but they may feel told off.

**Change:** at beat 7, Sensei says it out loud. TUT-C1 already nearly does this; see TUT-C1 edit 1.

And the plan's line "the Brute's red circle means the same thing" should read "the red circle always means: something is coming. You can step out, or Counter." Practicing a signal in the same form it shows up later helps it carry over (transfer-appropriate processing: Morris, Bransford & Franks 1977, *JVLVB* 16:519-533). **MODERATE**: a lab memory finding, applied here by analogy.

## TUT-C1 lines check (#518)

**Passes.** Every line is 8 words or fewer, kind and hopeful, and none of them are scary. The beat 7 line "Red circle, like the stones! Tap COUNTER!" already bridges the stones and the Brute, which covers my main catch. Edits for Content:

1. **Beat 7 tip, small change:** make the change of answer clear: "Red circle again! This time, tap COUNTER!" (7 words). "Like the stones" can make a kid step out instead.
2. **Beat 2, add one help line** for the slower retry after 2 missed waves (beat 2 rule d): "Watch the red. Step where I point." (7 words).
3. **Beat 8 success:** "Quick feet!" is already the stones' success line. Suggest "Nice DASH! Lots of room now." (6 words), so each skill gets its own praise.
4. **Beat 11 close:** "You began." reads oddly for a 6-year-old, and the close should point back to class. Suggest "Train in class. Your belt opens the Gate." (8 words).

The opening cards, the stumble line and the other tips and success lines are fine as written. The story wording still waits on Jay (TUT-P1 DECIDE 1).

## Load check (for a 6-year-old)

- New things: about 7 skills in 6-8 minutes, one at a time, each tried before the next. That's fine because they are all the core loop, which section 13 counts as the first session's job, not as "new systems".
- Text: 4 cards + 12 tips + 12 success lines + stumble + close is about 30 short lines. Fine at 8 words each, but success lines can be a cheer sound plus 2-3 words ("Nice!", "You did it!") so the kid isn't reading after every beat.
- No fear, guilt or pressure anywhere in the plan. Nothing in it competes with real training.
