# Creed and FAITH DOES verses through play (L5 proposal)

## Delivery Note
- **ITEM:** L5 (feeds C5, owned by the Content Designer).
- **WHAT:** Seven ways kids meet the Class Creed, the belt verses, covenants and FAITH DOES life words inside play. No tests, quizzes, locks, streaks or pressure. All words come from `docs/faith-and-creed.md` and are never reworded.
- **WHY:** Section 6 of `docs/faith-and-creed.md` says students learn these through play (Sensei, the Belt Gate screen, a Creed Wall), and they are not Gate locks. Hearing the words often, spaced out, in warm moments helps kids remember them without making it a test.

- **NEXT:** After Jay chooses, the Content Designer writes the C5 lines in the chosen places. I check that the pacing fits the section 13 unlock schedule.

## Ground rules used
- Text is exact. Per section 6, the game uses KJV wording where section 3 differs (Romans 3:23 "come short of the glory of God", Romans 5:8 "Christ died for us"). This draft quotes no verse text, so nothing here can drift. C5 copies the text from the doc.
- Nothing faith-related ever blocks progress, takes away power, or has a wrong answer. Power still comes from real training (sign-offs, stripes, practice).
- At most one new thing per session (section 13). Every faith moment can be replayed and skipped.
- No real student data. Ages 6+: every line can be read aloud with text-to-speech.

## Evidence key
STRONG = many studies or reviews agree. MODERATE = good studies, but not about this exact use. WEAK = theory or a small number of studies; treat as a reasonable guess.

Sources used below:
- Spacing effect: Cepeda, Pashler, Vul, Wixted & Rohrer (2006), *Psychological Bulletin*, meta-analysis of distributed practice. **STRONG** for spaced exposure helping memory. *(Cited from memory; check before Jay relies on it.)*
- Retrieval practice: Roediger & Karpicke (2006), *Psychological Science*. **STRONG** that recalling beats rereading, but it is usually done as testing. Here it's used only as optional "say it with Sensei" moments, which is a weaker form, so **MODERATE** for our use.
- Overjustification / rewards: Deci, Koestner & Ryan (1999), *Psychological Bulletin* meta-analysis. **STRONG** that expected, tangible rewards for an activity can lower kids' own interest. This is why the verses earn no power.
- Choral and rhythmic recitation helping kids remember text (songs, chants): research exists but is mixed. **WEAK**. I don't have a specific study I can cite with confidence, so none is named.
- Collection as motivation (completing a set): mostly design practice, not research. **WEAK**.

## Ideas

### 1. The Bow says the Creed (Home Dojo bow, first session)
**Idea:** When the student bows in the Home Dojo, they can choose to hold the bow, and Sensei and the class say one line of the Class Creed with them, shown big and read aloud. Each bow plays the next line. After the last line comes "I AM MORE THAN A CONQUEROR THROUGH JESUS CHRIST!!!" with a small celebration.
**Teaches:** The Class Creed, a line at a time, the same way it's said together in class.
**Why fun:** It's short, it feels like class, and the big shout at the end is a payoff kids already know.
**Evidence:** Spacing (Cepeda 2006): STRONG for short, repeated exposure. Choral speaking: WEAK.
**Cost:** S. **System:** Home Dojo bow, Sensei's Lessons. **Unlock:** First session (the bow is already in the first session).

### 2. Belt Gate verse card (Belt Gate / promotion moment)
**Idea:** When a real promotion is approved, the Belt Gate screen shows that belt's verse, covenant and life-word letter as a calm ceremony card, read aloud. It celebrates the student after the promotion. Nothing is needed before the promotion, and nothing is checked.
**Teaches:** The verse, covenant and life word for the belt just earned, tied to a proud moment.
**Why fun:** It makes the promotion feel like a ceremony. The new letter lights up, which leads into idea 3.
**Evidence:** Linking a memory to an emotional moment: MODERATE (general memory research; no specific study cited here). No reward is attached (Deci 1999: STRONG).
**Cost:** S. **System:** Gate and Path card, reward moments. **Unlock:** First promotion after the student starts playing (already part of the first-session Gate).

### 3. The Creed Wall: FAITH DOES letters
**Idea:** A wall in the Home Dojo with the nine empty letters F-A-I-T-H D-O-E-S. Each real promotion places one letter, with its life word and verse reference. Tapping a letter replays that belt's card (idea 2). Existing students see their earned letters during Welcome Week.
**Teaches:** The life words in order, and that the journey to black belt spells FAITH DOES.
**Why fun:** Kids love finishing a set, and it shows their real progress.
**Evidence:** Collection motivation: WEAK. Rereading the card when the student chooses gives spaced exposure (Cepeda 2006): STRONG.
**Cost:** M. **System:** Home Dojo. **Unlock:** Shows up when the first letter is earned (the first promotion), so it adds no first-session load. *Named in section 6, but please confirm it's in launch scope (question 1).*

### 4. Sensei's word of the room (proposal)
**Idea:** Between rooms, Sensei sometimes says a one-sentence thought on the current belt's life word, tied to what just happened ("You forgave yourself for that miss and tried again. That's F, Forgiveness."). The text is new Sensei dialogue, not scripture, so Jay approves it.
**Teaches:** What the life word means in action.
**Why fun:** It notices what the kid just did. It's praise, not a lesson.
**Evidence:** Connecting a value to the student's own behavior: WEAK to MODERATE. No source named.
**Cost:** S–M (writing). **System:** Sensei lines, run flow. **Unlock:** 2nd belt or later, so run 1 stays focused on Move, Strike, "!" and Counter.

### 5. Say it with Sensei (optional, proposal)
**Idea:** On the Creed Wall, a "Say it with me" button plays the verse line by line with a gap, and the student can say it out loud. No microphone, no scoring, no "wrong". Sensei always says "Well said" at the end.
**Teaches:** Recalling the verse from memory, privately.
**Why fun:** It feels like practicing for class with a friend, and you can't fail.
**Evidence:** Retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke 2006) is STRONG in testing form; this un-scored version is MODERATE at best.
**Cost:** S. **System:** Creed Wall. **Unlock:** With idea 3.

### 6. Covenant quest moments (proposal)
**Idea:** In 3rd-belt life-lesson quest stories, a choice that matches a covenant (for example, telling the truth) gets a gentle Sensei note naming that covenant. Every choice still moves the story on.
**Teaches:** Covenants as real choices with people.
**Why fun:** It's part of the story, not a sidebar.
**Evidence:** WEAK (story-based moral learning is studied, but I won't name a study I can't confirm).
**Cost:** M. **System:** Life-lesson quests. **Unlock:** 3rd belt (where quests unlock).

### 7. Black belt "FAITH DOES" finale (proposal)
**Idea:** When the S letter is placed, the wall plays all nine words in order, then the black belt verse (John 6:40) card.
**Teaches:** The whole set as one story.
**Why fun:** A big, earned finale.
**Evidence:** WEAK.
**Cost:** S. **System:** Creed Wall. **Unlock:** Black belt.
